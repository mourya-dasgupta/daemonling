Problem

  • Solo developers have no manager, no standup, no team rhythm.
  • Focus erodes quietly. Overwork is invisible until it hurts.
  • Timers count minutes. Trackers feel like surveillance. Neither makes you care.
Indie hacker after a day job, freelancer with three codebases, student shipping a side project. They know what to do; the missing piece is a reason to care today. [Add a beta interview quote.]

Insight

Responsibility beats rewardPeople protect what depends on them more reliably than they chase points.
Developers already emit focus signalsSessions, commits, green CI, editor focus — no manual logging.
Overwork is also neglectA caring pet gets tired after hours without a break, not just hungry.
Consumer focus pets prove the first point. The second is our wedge. The third is our ethics: we reward balance, not maximum minutes.

Why now

  • Focus pets went mainstream: Focus Friend hit #1 on the US App Store (Aug 2025), ~$370K estimated net revenue in that period — then fell to #48 within two weeks. ~1.4M downloads by early 2026.
  • Self-care pets monetise: Finch, ~$1M estimated monthly revenue; Finch Plus ~$40/year.
  • Developers are a big, growing audience: 180M+ on GitHub, 36M+ new in one year.
  • Developers want companions in their tools: vscode-pets (decorative) has ~3.5K stars.

Third-party estimates. Sources: Appfigures · Foundry · Adapty · GitHub Octoverse 2025 · vscode-pets

The Focus Friend curve is validation and warning: novelty spikes; retention is the game. Our answer is living inside the daily workflow — terminal, editor, git — instead of a phone app you must remember.
